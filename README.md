# Avalon

The Resistance: Avalon, played from phones around one table. One person creates a room, and everyone else joins with the 4-letter code or an invite link.

## Playing

- **5 to 15 players.** Up to 10, quest sizes, the evil count, and the rule that quest 4 needs two Fail cards with 7+ players all match the box. The box stops at 10, so 11 to 15 use house rules. They keep about a third of the table evil, and grow the teams so a quest still tends to include a traitor:

  | Players | Evil | Quest teams |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 11 | 4 | 3, 4, 4, 5, 5 |
  | 12 | 5 | 3, 4, 4, 5, 5 |
  | 13 | 5 | 4, 4, 5, 5, 6 |
  | 14 | 5 | 4, 5, 5, 6, 6 |
  | 15 | 6 | 4, 5, 5, 6, 6 |

  Quest 4 still needs two Fail cards. The lobby says when a table is on house rules, and the round table shrinks its seats to fit.
- **Role setups:** the host picks one before dealing.
  - **Merlin & Assassin**
  - **Full set:** every special role that fits the player count. Evil roles are added in the order Morgana, Mordred, Oberon, until the evil seats run out. So Oberon only appears with 10 or more players.
  - **Custom:** pick roles yourself.
  - **No special roles**

  A role that needs another (Percival needs Merlin, Morgana needs Percival, Mordred needs Merlin) switches off with it, because on its own it would change nothing.
- **Lady of the Lake (optional):** used after quests 2, 3 and 4. Only the holder sees the result. Players who have held her can't be examined.
- **Assassination:** if good wins three quests, evil is revealed and the Assassin gets one guess at Merlin.
- **Timers (optional):** for groups that like a faster pace. In the lobby the host can set a *talk timer* (2, 3 or 5 minutes for the leader to pick a team, and for evil to name Merlin) and a *vote timer* (30 seconds, 1 or 2 minutes). Every phone and the TV count down together. When time runs out the table sees *Time's up* and the players it's waiting on get a buzz, but nothing is played for anyone: the timer is a nudge, not a rule.

## Learning the game

New players can tap **Learn to play** on the home page, or the **?** button in any room, for an eight-step walkthrough. It includes a practice table: you pick a team, vote with the real tokens and see the reveal, and play a quest card as loyal or evil (and see why a loyal player can't play Fail). Nothing in the practice touches the real game, so it's safe to open mid-round.

## The screen

Each room is one tabletop scene rather than a page of boxes.

- **Two looks.** The host picks the room's look in the lobby, and can change it at any point. **Night** (the default) is the lake at night with an oak and brass table. **Parchment** draws everything as printed game pieces on cream paper: boards with perforated stamp edges, Approve and Reject as blackletter ballots, quest cards framed in blue and red, players as shields with their names on plaques, and forest green for your actions. Only colours, fonts and backgrounds change: the layout is identical in both. Every phone and the TV follow the host's choice, and both looks show exactly the same information. A room link opens straight in the room's look (the join and loading screens included), and each device remembers the last look it showed for its home page.
- **The Round Table.** Seats run clockwise in turn order. The tabletop holds the five quest medallions and the rejected-team track. Tap a finished medallion to see who went and how many Fail cards were played.
- **Your hand.** Everything you can do right now sits at the foot of the table: proposing a team, the Approve/Reject tokens, the two quest cards, the Lady, the Assassin's choice. On laptops it sits beside the table so nothing scrolls out of view.
- **Seating to match the room.** When everyone is at one table, the host taps *Rearrange players* in the lobby and drags each seat to where that person is really sitting, going clockwise (or taps a player, then the seat they belong in). *Save seating* shows the new order to everyone, and roles are dealt in it, so the leader's crown passes around the table the same way it would in the box game.
- **Live team building.** As the leader taps seats, everyone watches the shields appear. The team forms in front of the table instead of arriving all at once.
- **Last vote on the seats.** While a quest is underway, or after a team is rejected, each seat shows how that player voted. Reading votes is most of the game, so the votes stay where people are looking.
- **Player records.** Tap anyone to see every vote they cast, the quests they joined, and how many of those failed. You can mark them **Trust** or **Suspect**. Your marks are private and stay on your phone for that game only.
- **Avatars.** Pick a crest (helm, crown, crossed swords, tower, rose, moon, owl, wolf, stag, raven and more) and one of 15 colours before you create or join a room, or keep your initials. No two players in a room share a crest or a colour: ones already taken show greyed out in the picker, and if yours is taken when you join you get your initials or a free colour instead, then can pick again from what's left. The crests are drawn in the same line style as the role cards, so they look identical on every phone. Your phone remembers the choice for next time. Tap your own seat at any point to change it.
- **Back on a new device.** If a phone dies or someone switches devices mid-game, the host taps their seat and makes a 6-letter rejoin code. On the new device the player opens the room link, chooses *Back on a new device*, and enters it: they're back in their seat with their role, and the old device is signed out so a seat is never on two phones. Codes work once, for 10 minutes, and only the person who made one can see it. If the host is the one who's gone, anyone can make a code for a player who hasn't been seen for over a minute.
- **Removing players.** In the lobby, the host taps a player's seat and taps *Remove* twice. The second tap is there so nobody gets kicked by a stray touch. The removed player is told why they're back at the join screen, and can rejoin with the code while the room is still in the lobby. Players can't be removed mid-game, because their seat holds a secret role; the host can end the game early instead.
- **Ending a game early.** The host has *End game early* at the foot of their panel in every phase (and in the chronicle drawer). It takes a second tap to confirm, then everyone returns to the lobby. A game ended early doesn't count towards the scores.
- **Who's here, and whose turn it is.** A seat fades and says *away* when that phone hasn't checked in for 45 seconds. The panel under the table names who the game is waiting on ("Waiting on Cai and Bors (away)"), so a stalled vote has an obvious culprit. When it becomes your turn your phone buzzes, and if Avalon is in a background tab its title flashes *Your turn*. The chronicle drawer lists every player with their status; tap one to open their card from any phase, even while you're the leader picking a team (when tapping a seat picks it instead).
- **TV view.** Put the table on a shared screen from any device, before or during a game. On the TV or laptop, go to `/tv` and type the room code, or tap *Show it on a TV* on the home page after entering the code. A room link opened on a device that isn't playing also offers *Just watching? Show this table on a TV*, even once the game has started. Players can open it on their own device from the lobby or the chronicle drawer, which also shows the address to type on another screen. In the lobby it shows a QR code and the room code so people can scan in. During the game it shows the round table, the quest board, who it's waiting on, recent table talk, and the vote and quest reveals in big type. It only ever receives public information (the same as watching the table), needs no seat, and keeps the screen awake.
- **Scores across games.** The room keeps a running tally, like "Good 3 – Evil 2", shown in the lobby, at the end of each game and on the TV. Open *Player standings* in the lobby to see how many games each player has won. Only finished games count: a game the host ends early doesn't. The host can reset the tally from the standings.
- **End-of-game recap.** When a game ends, tap *See the recap*. It shows who was on which side and what role they had, every quest with the team that went, how the table voted for it, which knights on it were evil, and how many teams were rejected first. It also covers each Lady of the Lake result and who the Assassin named.
- **Reactions.** Thumbs up, thumbs down, hmm, side-eye, shock and laughter pop up over your seat for the whole table, for when a vote goes sideways. They're drawn in the same line style as the crests rather than emoji, so they look the same on every phone.
- **Your role** is dealt face down. Tap to turn it over, so you choose when to look.
- **Table talk** (chat) is docked on wide screens and slides in from the side on phones. Each name is written in a light version of that player's avatar colour (on paper, the colour itself), so you can match a line to a seat at a glance. **The chronicle** keeps every proposal, vote and quest result, plus the roles in play. Wherever votes are shown (the reveal after each vote, the chronicle and the recap), each player appears with their avatar, so you can read the split at a glance.

### What stays secret

The server decides what each phone receives, so nobody can find another player's role in the browser's developer tools. Votes stay hidden until everyone has voted, and quest cards are shuffled so only the number of Fails is shown. Good and evil players see the same two quest cards: a glance at someone's screen gives nothing away, and the server rejects a Fail from a loyal player.

## Install it like an app

Avalon can be added to a phone's home screen, where it opens full-screen with its own icon and no browser bar. On Android and in Chrome or Edge, tap **Install the app** on the home page. On an iPhone, the home page explains the Safari steps (Share, then Add to Home Screen). It's still the same website underneath, so updates arrive by themselves.

## Run it locally

```bash
npm install
npm run dev
```

Open http://localhost:3000. Locally, rooms are kept in memory, so no database is needed. Other devices on your Wi-Fi can join at `http://<your-computer's-IP>:3000`.

To check the rules, run `npm run simulate` in a second terminal while the dev server is running. It plays five complete games through the API: different player counts, an assassination that hits and one that misses, five rejections, and three failed quests. It checks that hidden information never leaks.

## Deploy to Vercel

Vercel functions don't share memory, so rooms are stored in Upstash Redis (free tier).

1. Import this repository at vercel.com/new.
2. In the Vercel project, open **Storage**, create a Redis database (Upstash for Redis or Vercel's own Redis, both have free plans) and connect it to the project. Tick every environment you deploy to (Production, and Preview if you use preview links).
3. **Redeploy.** A deployment only gets the variables that existed when it was built, so connecting the store isn't enough on its own.

The app finds Redis by itself. It accepts Upstash's REST variables (`KV_REST_API_URL` + `KV_REST_API_TOKEN` or `UPSTASH_REDIS_REST_URL` + `UPSTASH_REDIS_REST_TOKEN`, with or without a custom prefix) or a `redis://` connection string in `REDIS_URL`. REST is preferred because it needs no open connection.

If storage still isn't reachable, creating a room shows an error. It names the environment (production or preview) and lists which Redis-related variable names the deployment can see, without their values. "No Redis variables at all" means the store isn't connected to that environment or the deployment predates it. "Can see X but no usable…" means the store is connected but its variables are in an unexpected form.

Rooms expire 12 hours after their last change. Phones check for updates every 1.5 seconds while things are happening, slowing to 3 seconds during long discussions, 2.5 to 4 seconds in the lobby, and 8 seconds in a background tab.

Redis usage stays low however many people are playing:
- Polls that land on the same server instance share one read for up to a second.
- Each phone's "I'm here" check-in goes to a small separate record about every 25 seconds, so it costs one command and doesn't force everyone to re-download the room.

Measured with 15 phones polling at the fastest rate, a game uses about 3 Redis commands a second, around 11k an hour. Upstash's free tier (500k a month) covers roughly 40 hours of 15-player play a month. If Vercel spreads traffic over several instances, multiply by their number.
